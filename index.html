How it works

Two inputs arrive in parallel: the policy's action chunk and a per-hand contact predictor on the wrist camera. RoboPace turns the chunk into a joint path, marks the waypoints where a contact is imminent, and solves for a new timing that is fast elsewhere and held at the contact speed limit there.

Main result

On a dual-arm robot and five commands, RoboPace has the highest success rate on four of five, matching the reliability of the slow uniform rate in about half its time. Uniformly faster execution fails at contact; uniformly slower execution is reliable but slow.

Paper Abstract

Robot manipulation data has been shifting from teleoperation toward demonstrations, through robot-free interfaces such as Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) or directly from human hands. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies trained on such data inherit the demonstrator's timing. Yet human timing does not directly transfer to robots: compliant hands tolerate fast contact, whereas robots may overshoot under actuator and tracking limits; conversely, robots can move faster in free space. This motivates a unified approach that reconciles execution speed with contact safety. We present RoboPace, an online retiming layer that preserves the policy's geometric path while adapting its timing to the kinematic and dynamic constraints of the target robot. It adapts execution speed based on predicted contact, jointly accounting for contact-dependent speed limits and the robot's motion constraints. The method requires no policy retraining and operates in real time. Across three contact-rich tasks on a dual-arm robot, we find that RoboPace achieves higher success than a high-success-rate uniform baseline while roughly halving execution time, yielding a better speed–success trade-off than the baselines.
